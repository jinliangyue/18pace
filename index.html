What 1:50 actually feels like (from someone who ran it)

A calculator can give you splits. It can't tell you about the wall at km 16, or the moment your legs lock up at km 19. Here's the real race-day data.

The first 10K: Holding back is hard

Every instinct tells you to go out at 5:00/km. Don't. The first 10K should feel embarrassingly easy at 5:20-5:30/km. If you're not holding back, you'll pay for it at km 17.

The 15-18K stretch: The wall is real

This is where most people break. Your gel timing matters: take your first gel at km 8-9, second at km 13-14. Skip the gel and your pace will drop 30+ seconds in the final 5K.

The final 3K: Survive, then sprint

At km 18, you won't want to speed up. Your brain will lie to you. Hold your target pace. If you have anything left in the final 500m, use it. The clock rewards every second under 1:50.

The 1:50 race-day strategy

A simple, repeatable plan. Tested in actual sub-1:50 finishes.

1

Negative split the first half

Start at 5:20-5:30/km for the first 10K. Pick up to 5:10/km for the second half.

2

Gel at km 8-9 and km 13-14

Don't wait until you feel hungry. Your glycogen is depleting long before your stomach notices.

3

Water at every station after km 7

Even if you're not thirsty. Dehydration in the back half is what kills your pace.

4

Hold target pace from km 18 to 20

The hardest 2K of your life. Stay disciplined. Sprint the final 1.1K.

Frequently asked questions

What pace do I need for a 1:50 half marathon? ↓

You need to average approximately 5:13 per kilometer (8:24 per mile) to finish a half marathon in 1 hour 50 minutes.

Is 1:50 a good half marathon time? ↓

1:50 is a strong amateur time. It places most finishers in the top 10-15% of their age group globally, and requires consistent training over 3-6 months.

How many miles is a 1:50 half marathon? ↓

A half marathon is 13.1 miles (21.1 km) regardless of finish time.
